Animal Welfare & Rabies Tracking

QR collar tracking for street dogs across Sri Lanka.

PawTag is a platform for tracking street dog vaccination, sterilisation, and medical treatment via a QR-coded collar. When an animal is tagged in the field, anyone can scan the collar to check rabies vaccination dates, medical history, or reach the clinic caring for the dog.

Used by veterinarians, animal-welfare field workers, community members, and pet owners to keep records clear and accessible.

Log in Scan a collar

Scanning a collar is public and does not require an account or app download.

How it works

A physical QR collar connects dogs on the road directly to their veterinary history.

1

Tag and register in the field

During sterilisation or vaccination drives, field workers fit a numbered QR collar on the dog and log photos, distinguishing marks, location, and rabies batch numbers into PawTag.

2

Scan the collar anytime

If someone finds an injured or lost dog, or a team checks an area during a booster round, scanning the collar immediately shows whether the dog is vaccinated and when the booster is due.

3

Record treatments and notes

Veterinarians and authorized welfare staff update sterilisation records, wound treatments, and medication notes so everyone working in that district sees the same history.

Who it's for

Built for the practical needs of people working with animals on the ground.

Field workers & welfare groups

Manage sterilisation drives, keep accurate counts of street dogs in your coverage area, and avoid duplicate vaccinations during catch-neuter-vaccinate-release operations.

Veterinarians & clinics

Look up a dog's past medical interventions, log treatments and rabies batch certificates, and give rescuers a reliable record when following up on surgeries.

Community members & pet owners

Check if a neighborhood community dog has been vaccinated, reach the registered feeder or rescue team if a dog is sick, or keep health records for adopted street dogs.

Designed for real conditions

Field work doesn't always happen with high-speed internet or desktop computers.

Instant public scanning

Anyone with a standard smartphone camera can scan a collar. The animal's public status loads in their mobile browser without installing an app, creating an account, or waiting for approvals.

Offline support for field rounds

When teams are in areas with poor mobile signals, recently viewed records and cached animal data stay accessible so vaccinations and IDs can still be checked.
